Chapter 9›9.1 Overview of Big Data
SECTION 9.1

Overview of Big Data

Let's start from the very beginning. No tech jargon, just plain language — and things you already know.

First — What even IS data?

Data is simply recorded information. Any fact, number, word, image, or signal that can be stored and read is data. You've been working with data your whole life — you just never called it that.

📋
Structured Data
Neatly organized in rows and columns. Easy for computers to read and search.
Examples: Your bank statement, an Excel spreadsheet, a student grade list
📧
Semi-Structured Data
Partly organized. Has some structure but also contains free-form content.
Examples: Emails (sender + date = structured, message body = not), WhatsApp messages, JSON files
🎬
Unstructured Data
No fixed format. Hardest to process but makes up 80% of all data in the world.
Examples: TikTok videos, Instagram photos, voice notes, PDF documents

So what makes data "BIG"?

Big Data is not just about file size. It means data that is so large, fast, or complex that normal tools like Excel or a regular database can't handle it.

🏟️
Think of it like a football stadium vs a classroom
A classroom has 30 students. A teacher can track their names, marks, and attendance easily in a notebook. That's regular data.

Now imagine you need to track every single movement, heartbeat, food purchase, phone tap, and step taken by 50,000 fans inside a stadium — every second, for 90 minutes. That's Big Data. You'd need entirely different tools, storage systems, and processing power just to handle it.

The 5 V's of Big Data

Researchers at IBM and Gartner defined Big Data using these five characteristics. If data has all five — it's definitely Big Data.

📦
V
Volume
The amount of data. We're not talking gigabytes — we're talking petabytes and zettabytes.
📌 Facebook stores 100+ petabytes of photos. That's 100 million gigabytes.
⚡
V
Velocity
The speed at which data is created and must be processed — often in real time.
📌 Google processes 8.5 billion searches per day. That's nearly 100,000 every second.
🎭
V
Variety
Data comes in many formats — text, images, videos, audio, sensor readings, GPS logs.
📌 One single Instagram post has text, photo, location, hashtags, time, and device info.
✅
V
Veracity
How trustworthy and accurate the data is. Bad data = wrong decisions.
📌 If someone enters a fake birthday online, that data is wrong. Veracity asks: "Can we trust this?"
💡
V
Value
The usefulness extracted from data. Raw data means nothing until it becomes insight.
📌 Netflix uses viewing data to recommend your next show — that's turning raw data into value.

Key Concept Big Data is not just a storage problem — it's a processing problem. The challenge is making sense of massive, diverse, fast-moving data fast enough for it to be useful. That's why we need specialised technologies.

Chapter 9›9.2 Big Data in Businesses
SECTION 9.2

Big Data in Businesses

This isn't a future concept. Companies you use every single day are already making billions using Big Data. Let's look at how.

📖

Before we dive in — what do businesses actually DO with big data? They collect data from their customers, run it through analytical tools, find patterns, and use those patterns to make better decisions — about products, prices, marketing, operations, and more.

🎬 Entertainment & Streaming
🛒 Retail & E-Commerce
🏥 Healthcare
🎬 Netflix — Entertainment
80%
of shows you watch come from Netflix's recommendation engine
How Netflix Knows What You'll Watch Next
Every time you use Netflix, it watches you back — in a data sense. It records what you watch, how long, when you pause, what you rewind, and what you skip. It does this for 260 million+ subscribers globally.

Netflix feeds all this data into its recommendation algorithm. That's why your homepage looks different from your friend's — it's personalised to your taste. This recommendation system saves Netflix an estimated $1 billion+ per year in customer retention.
💡 Netflix even used data to decide to make "House of Cards" — data showed users loved the UK version AND Kevin Spacey movies. So they combined both.
🛒 Amazon — Retail
35%
of Amazon's revenue comes from its recommendation engine
Amazon Knows What You'll Buy Before You Do
Amazon tracks every product you view, every review you read, every item you put in your cart (even if you don't buy). It uses this to show you "Customers who bought this also bought..." — which drives billions in extra sales.

Amazon's predictive logistics system even pre-ships items to local warehouses before you click "buy," anticipating your purchase based on your browsing patterns.
💡 Anticipatory shipping — Amazon sometimes dispatches your order before you've placed it, just with your address left blank.
🚗 Grab & Uber — Ride-Hailing
Real-time
pricing updated every few minutes using live data
Why Grab Costs More During Rain
Grab and Uber use Big Data to calculate "surge pricing" — when lots of people want rides but few drivers are available, the price goes up automatically. This isn't a human making a decision. It's an algorithm processing millions of data points (GPS locations, demand signals, weather, traffic) every second.

They also use data to predict where demand will spike next — positioning drivers before the rush even starts.
💡 Next time Grab charges you extra during KLIA rush hour — that's Big Data pricing at work.

Big Data Saves Lives — Healthcare

One of the most powerful applications of Big Data is in medicine. Hospitals are now using predictive analytics to help patients before they even get sick.

🏥 Massachusetts General Hospital — USA
22%
reduction in patients being re-admitted to hospital
Predicting Who Will Get Sick Again
Doctors used Big Data to analyse thousands of patient records — age, medical history, medication, lifestyle. They built a model that predicts which patients are likely to be readmitted within 30 days of leaving hospital.

Nurses could then call those patients early, adjust medications, or schedule checkups — before an emergency happened. This saved lives AND reduced healthcare costs significantly.
💡 The model doesn't replace doctors — it helps them focus their attention on the right patients at the right time.
🏦
🏦 JPMorgan Chase — Banking
360K
hours of legal work done in seconds using AI
Reading 12,000 Legal Contracts in Seconds
JPMorgan developed an AI tool called COiN (Contract Intelligence) that reads and analyses 12,000 commercial credit contracts in a matter of seconds. Previously, this took lawyers 360,000 hours per year to do manually.

The bank also uses Big Data to detect fraud — scanning millions of transactions in real-time and flagging anything unusual before money leaves your account.
💡 Your bank already uses Big Data every time you make a purchase — to check if it looks like it's really you.

Other Examples You'll Recognise

🎵
Spotify
Analyses your listening history to create your personalised "Discover Weekly" playlist every Monday. It knows your mood from the tempo and genre of what you play.
📱
TikTok & Instagram
Track every second you spend on each video. If you watched 40% of a cat video and 95% of a cooking video, the algorithm learns your preferences and fills your feed accordingly.
🛍️
Lazada & Shopee
Use Big Data to show you items you're likely to buy based on your search history, location, time of day, and what similar customers purchased. Flash sale timing? Also data-driven.
🌦️
Weather Apps
Combine satellite data, sensor networks, historical records, and ocean temperature readings to forecast rain. Modern forecasts use petabytes of atmospheric data analysed every hour.
🏪
Supermarkets
Your loyalty card tracks every item you buy. Supermarkets use this to arrange store layouts, plan promotions, and send you targeted vouchers for products you actually buy.
✈️
Airlines
Flight prices change dozens of times per day based on Big Data — demand patterns, seat availability, competitor prices, and historical booking behaviour all feed into dynamic pricing engines.
✅

Bottom Line Every company that has customers is collecting data. The ones using it intelligently are growing faster, serving better, and spending less. That's the business case for Big Data in one sentence.

Chapter 9›9.3 Technologies for Handling Big Data
SECTION 9.3

Technologies for Handling Big Data

These are the tools that make Big Data possible. You don't need to become an engineer — but every business leader should know what these tools do.

🎯

Why can't we just use Excel? Excel can handle about 1 million rows. A single day of transaction data for a company like Grab has hundreds of millions of rows. We need completely different tools designed from the ground up for scale, speed, and complexity.

⚙️ Storage & Processing Tools

These tools are the backbone — they store huge amounts of data and process it quickly.

🐘
Storage & Processing
Apache Hadoop
An open-source framework that spreads large files across many computers and processes them in parallel. Instead of one super-powerful computer, you use hundreds of ordinary ones working together.
🔑 Analogy Instead of one giant librarian sorting all books, Hadoop is like having 500 librarians each sorting a section — much faster, and if one gets tired, the others keep going.
⚡
Fast Processing
Apache Spark
A faster version of Hadoop that processes data entirely in memory (RAM) instead of on disk. Up to 100× faster than Hadoop for certain tasks. Used when speed matters, like real-time fraud detection.
🔑 Analogy Hadoop is like reading a book from a library shelf. Spark is like having the whole book already memorised — lightning fast to search.
🌊
Real-time Streaming
Apache Kafka
A system that moves data from where it's created to where it needs to be processed — in real time. Used by Uber, LinkedIn, and Netflix to handle millions of events per second continuously.
🔑 Analogy Kafka is like a conveyor belt at a supermarket. Products (data) keep moving from one end to the other without stopping — no backlog, no waiting.
☁️
Cloud Storage
Amazon S3 / Google Cloud
Instead of buying physical servers, businesses rent cloud storage from Amazon or Google. It's infinitely scalable, pay-as-you-use, and accessible from anywhere in the world.
🔑 Analogy Renting cloud storage is like renting a unit at a storage facility instead of building your own warehouse. You only pay for what you use, and they handle maintenance.
🗄️
Databases
SQL & NoSQL Databases
SQL (like MySQL) is great for structured, neatly organised data — like bank transactions. NoSQL (like MongoDB) handles flexible, unstructured data at massive scale — like social media posts or product catalogues.
🔑 Analogy SQL is a filing cabinet with labelled folders. NoSQL is a big bin where you throw anything in — messier, but far more flexible.
🏗️
Data Warehousing
Google BigQuery / Snowflake
Cloud-based warehouses designed to run complex queries on billions of rows in seconds. Used by analysts and business managers to ask questions like "Which product sold the most in Southeast Asia last quarter?"
🔑 Analogy BigQuery is like Google Search — but for your company's data. Ask a question, get an answer in seconds from billions of records.

📊 Analytics & Visualisation Tools

These are the tools that turn processed data into charts, dashboards, and insights that business leaders can actually read and act on.

📊
Business Intelligence
Tableau
A drag-and-drop tool that creates beautiful, interactive charts and dashboards without writing code. Widely used in business analyst and management roles. Connect your data → drag fields → see your chart.
🔑 Who uses it Sales managers tracking performance. Marketing teams visualising campaign results. Finance teams monitoring budgets in real time.
📈
Business Intelligence
Microsoft Power BI
Microsoft's analytics platform that integrates directly with Excel and Office 365. If you can use Excel, you can learn Power BI. The most commonly required BI tool in job postings across Southeast Asia.
🔑 Career tip Learning Power BI is one of the highest-ROI skills for any business student. It appears in thousands of job listings from analyst to management roles.
🐍
Data Science & AI
Python
The world's most popular programming language for data analysis, machine learning, and AI. With libraries like Pandas and NumPy, Python can process datasets that would crash Excel in seconds.
🔑 Who uses it Data scientists, data analysts, machine learning engineers. Not required for most management roles — but a major career advantage if you have it.
🧩

How These Tools Work Together A retail company collects transaction data → stored in Amazon S3 → cleaned using Apache Spark → moved into Snowflake (warehouse) → visualised in Power BI → managers see a dashboard showing sales trends by store. Each tool plays a specific role in the chain.

Chapter 9›9.4 Understanding Big Data Technology Foundation
SECTION 9.4

Understanding the Big Data Technology Foundation

Now let's put it all together. How do these technologies connect? What does a Big Data system actually look like end-to-end?

🔄 The Big Data Pipeline

All Big Data systems follow the same basic flow. Data travels from where it's created, through a series of steps, until it becomes a useful insight. Think of it like a food supply chain — from farm to table.

🏭
The Factory Assembly Line Analogy
Raw materials go in one end of a factory. Each station on the assembly line does something specific — cuts, shapes, refines, and assembles. A finished product comes out the other end.

The Big Data pipeline works the same way. Raw, messy data goes in. Each step cleans, processes, and enriches it. At the end, a clean, useful insight comes out — and a business manager uses that insight to make a better decision.
📡
1
COLLECT
Data is gathered from all sources — apps, sensors, websites, transactions, social media, GPS. It arrives continuously, often in real-time.
›
🗄️
2
STORE
Raw data lands in a Data Lake — a giant storage pool for all formats of data. Think of it as a reservoir. No processing yet, just safe storage at scale.
›
⚙️
3
PROCESS
Data is cleaned (remove errors, fix formats), filtered (keep what's relevant), and transformed (standardise structure). This is called ETL — Extract, Transform, Load.
›
🧠
4
ANALYSE
Clean data moves into a Data Warehouse. Algorithms, AI models, and analysts query the data — looking for patterns, trends, correlations, and anomalies.
›
🎯
5
DECIDE
Insights are presented in dashboards and reports. Business leaders use them to make smarter, faster, evidence-based decisions — instead of guessing.

Source: IBM Data & AI Framework · Gartner Data Architecture Guide

🌊 Data Lake vs 🏗️ Data Warehouse

These two are often confused. They serve different purposes in the Big Data pipeline — here's a simple way to understand both.

🌊
Data Lake
"Store everything, figure it out later"
  • Stores raw, unprocessed data in any format
  • Can hold structured AND unstructured data
  • Very cheap to store large volumes
  • Used by data engineers and scientists
  • Think: a giant pond where all water (data) flows into
🏗️
Data Warehouse
"Cleaned, organised, ready to use"
  • Stores processed, structured, ready-to-query data
  • Optimised for fast analytics and reporting
  • Used by analysts and business intelligence teams
  • Expensive but very fast for querying
  • Think: a well-organised factory with labelled shelves
💧

Simple way to remember the difference A Data Lake is like a huge natural lake — water of all types flows in. A Data Warehouse is like a bottled water factory — only clean, purified water in specific bottles. You need the lake to feed the factory.

🕐 Batch Processing vs Real-time Processing

Not all Big Data needs to be processed instantly. There are two main approaches — and the right one depends on the business need.

Feature 🕐 Batch Processing ⚡ Real-time Processing
What it is Processes large chunks of data at scheduled intervals (hourly, nightly, weekly) Processes data the moment it arrives — within milliseconds
When to use When you can wait for the answer — end-of-day reports, monthly invoices When delay is unacceptable — fraud detection, stock trading, live tracking
Cost Cheaper (you process in bulk, off-peak hours) More expensive (constant processing, always on)
Example Your bank statement is calculated and sent to you once a month Your bank blocks a suspicious transaction in real-time while you're at the checkout
Tools used Hadoop MapReduce, traditional ETL tools Apache Kafka, Apache Spark Streaming

🏛️ The Big Data Technology Architecture

Big Data systems are typically built in three layers — each one serving a different function. Think of it like a building: foundations on the bottom, working floors in the middle, and a viewing platform on top.

🔝
LAYER 3 — Presentation Layer
Dashboards, Reports & Visualisations
What business users and managers see. Tools: Tableau, Power BI, Google Looker Studio, custom web dashboards. This is where insights become decisions.
⚙️
LAYER 2 — Processing & Analytics Layer
Data Cleaning, Transformation & Analysis
Where data is processed and made sense of. Tools: Apache Spark, Python/R, SQL queries, Machine Learning models. This is where raw data becomes meaningful patterns.
🏗️
LAYER 1 — Infrastructure Layer (Foundation)
Storage, Collection & Ingestion
The base. Receives raw data from all sources and stores it reliably at scale. Tools: Hadoop, Apache Kafka, Amazon S3, Data Lakes. Without this foundation, nothing above can work.
🎓

Exam Tip Remember the three layers from bottom to top: Infrastructure → Processing → Presentation. Data flows upward — collected at the bottom, processed in the middle, presented at the top for decision-making. Each layer depends on the one below it.

☁️ The Role of Cloud Computing

Before cloud computing, building a Big Data system required buying and maintaining physical servers — extremely expensive and slow to set up. Cloud computing changed everything.

😓
Before Cloud — On-Premise
• Buy expensive physical servers
• Hire IT staff to manage them
• Takes months to set up
• Pay whether you use them or not
• Limited by physical space
😊
After Cloud — AWS / Azure / GCP
• Rent as much storage as you need
• Up and running in minutes
• Pay only for what you actually use
• Scale up or down instantly
• Available anywhere in the world
🌏

Real Impact A startup in Singapore can now build a Big Data system for the cost of a few hundred dollars per month using Amazon Web Services. Ten years ago, the same system would have required millions in hardware and years of setup. Cloud computing democratised Big Data — making it accessible to businesses of all sizes.

CHAPTER SUMMARY

Key Takeaways

If you remember nothing else from this chapter, remember these five things.

1
Big Data is defined by the 5 V's — Volume, Velocity, Variety, Veracity, and Value. Data becomes "big" when normal tools like Excel can't process it fast enough or at the scale required.
2
Every major business already uses Big Data — Netflix recommends shows, Amazon predicts purchases, banks detect fraud in real-time, hospitals predict which patients need extra care. It's everywhere.
3
Key technologies include Hadoop (storage at scale), Spark (fast processing), Kafka (real-time streaming), Cloud platforms (Amazon/Google/Microsoft), and BI tools like Tableau and Power BI for visualisation.
4
The Big Data pipeline follows five stages: Collect → Store → Process → Analyse → Decide. Raw, messy data goes in at one end; clean, useful business insights come out the other.
5
The technology foundation has three layers: Infrastructure (store data), Processing (make sense of data), and Presentation (display results). Cloud computing made this affordable for companies of all sizes.
